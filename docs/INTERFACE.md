# Architecture and maintenance

## Entry points and ownership

Elysium Story is a lazy-loaded optional controller in `ui/ElysiumStory.tsx`. It reuses `MarsFreeRoam` and the same `MarsWorld` instance through dialogs and the Earth prologue. `content/elysiumMissions.json` owns authored dialogue, choices and explanations; `game/elysiumMission.ts` owns deterministic transitions, resource coupling and seeded readiness faults. `elysiumSave.ts` validates a versioned local save. Story saves do not contain free-text input, account data or telemetry, and do not overwrite the autonomous rover save.

`game/elysiumWorld.ts` defines the optional scene bridge. Explicit resource commands carry a revision; the scene applies rover charge once and reports the applied revision with actual vehicle telemetry. Stale telemetry cannot undo a choice. Story charging obeys the selected allocation, while existing vehicle physics and Free Exploration charging remain unchanged. Mission station interactions use the real player position; doors retain priority and story panels also offer accessible station buttons. Small procedural signs and a destination pin identify the authored locations in `elysiumSites.ts`. No extra scene lights or external assets are introduced.

Both modes expose the radiation phone through P / Arrow Up or its touch launcher. A modal phone owns focus and clears world movement while open; Arrow Down, P or Escape closes it. Live NOAA near-Earth observations and NASA DONKI archive replays remain separate from simulated Mars exposure. Story passes its cumulative dose through `simulatedDose`; live observations cannot change mission resources, damage or readiness. Resource stages advance only through deliberate actions; retries retain completed missions and logs. Story mode and the bundled mission JSON do not load during Free Exploration.

`ui/MissionActivity.tsx` loads on demand inside the existing paused Story dialog. `game/missionActivities.ts` validates four distinct, isolated wire pairs and bounded normalized brush strokes against a 24×12 coverage grid. Cleaning requires 85% coverage overall and 70% in each quadrant; incremental pointer feedback uses the same geometry as final validation. No animation loop, downloaded art or dependency is added. Keyboard and touch use the same proof rules as mouse input.

The reducer accepts `complete-activity` for `solar-connect`, `solar-clean` and `clear-panels`; raw `task` actions cannot complete them. The controller requires actual solar-station proximity and the reducer validates prerequisites and evidence before changing progress. New or unfinished M2 requires initial cleaning before grid allocation. Version-1 saves retain completed missions and existing wire repair; completed M2 is not reopened. Practice replay grants no resources. Workbench strokes remain transient; cancellation returns to the station and does not save partial work. Repeated cleanup is available only when later dust actually exists, restoring future yield rather than stored power.

`game/trainingActivities.ts` defines 25 additional workbenches across M1–M7 and validates bounded placements, rotations, reference dials, allocations and ordered steps. The `complete-training` action validates evidence and applies the existing task/choice/fault effects atomically; converted raw actions cannot bypass the workbench. `trainingAvailable`, `trainingCompleted`, `trainingAllowedChoices` and `trainingForAction` keep launch buttons, practice and charge-limited plans consistent with the reducer. No new save fields or version migration are needed. Temporary workbench evidence never enters the save.

`TrainingWorkshop` and `LifeSupportGames` are loaded only when their station games open. Both live inside the existing paused Story dialog with its focus trap and Escape handling. The controller checks current physical station proximity on launch and submission. Fault repairs return to the night-test panel; other games return to the station. Planning games replace the old lists of technical choices and greenhouse forms; their results use the same authored resource presets. Long explanations, route details and resource reports are optional disclosures. Walking, rover travel and the original world interaction systems remain the way to reach each job.

`app/page.tsx` lazily loads `MarsFreeRoam`. `static-app/main.tsx` uses the same entry for GitHub Pages. All query strings enter the same current exploration game.

`MarsWorld` reports inspection IDs, view/rover state, readiness, WebGL failure, terrain mode, atlas requests and graphics-calibration status through callbacks. `createMarsWorld.ts` creates the terrain and science-rover targets. There is no hidden mission, lunar or outpost mode in this renderer.

`game/interiorCamera.ts` handles automatic eye view at the outpost and greenhouse boundaries. Entry remembers the outdoor preference; departure restores it after a 250 ms grace period to prevent doorway flicker. Camera position and orientation blend over 550 ms using cached vectors/quaternions and the existing wall constraints. Reduced motion completes the blend immediately. V/touch camera switching remains available indoors; the saved outdoor preference still returns on departure. Rover and landing cameras retain their existing paths. Debug diagnostics expose `cameraView`, `cameraBlend`, `indoorCamera` and `cameraPosition` for walkthrough checks.

`game/elysiumGuidance.ts` derives one actionable next job from existing saves, station proximity, rover charge and night-test state. The Story controller offers the next workbench at the current station and a focused mission-complete handoff after journaling. M3–M5 remain selectable in any order. Finished jobs are available under practice, and opening a guidance card never teleports or grants progress.

## Rendering and performance

The essential scene loads behind `StarshipArrival`. The 32-second opening shows a coastal Earth launch, an intact orbital coast, a covered interplanetary edit and a fictional Mars catch beside the actual campus. A single white cylindrical crew vehicle stays together, using a gentle upright descent, three/two/one-engine braking, arm closure and pin contact. The orbit shot holds if the world is not prepared yet. Skip queues safe entry; pause and hidden tabs stop the sequence. Reduced motion presents a stationary orbital shot while loading, then enters the game without a moving descent.

Earth and space use a temporary WebGL renderer with a maximum 1.25 pixel ratio and 1024 shadows; coarse-pointer/Performance devices use ratio 1 and no shadows. The Mars renderer prepares assets and shaders concurrently, then draws descent using its actual campus, terrain and persistent catch rig while the temporary renderer stops drawing. Simulation, Auto calibration and input remain suspended under the cover. Mars dust has 24 or 48 pooled slots. No extra real-time lights, external assets or render services are added. The opening releases its temporary GPU resources at handoff; failure falls through to the prepared game, with the existing game retry screen retained. The caught ship and tower remain beside the base, and the old shuttle stays as the existing shelter. Spawn position, final yaw and ground-relative camera height are unchanged. See [arrival architecture](ARRIVAL.md).

Auto starts light, prepares optional shaders after entry and samples ordinary animation frames. Each trial skips six settling frames and measures up to one second or 180 samples. A 43 FPS trial threshold provides headroom for the 40 FPS runtime target. Manual selection, pause dialogs, hidden/occluded tabs, preparation and unsettled resolution are excluded. There is no separate continuous benchmark render loop.

Auto can select Ultra on integrated graphics. Failed upgrades restore the best passing preset; slow Performance trials can lower render scale to 0.85, 0.70 or 0.55. The ongoing governor uses hysteresis and changes scale by at most 0.04 every 400 ms. Results are neither stored nor transmitted.

Outpost interiors use a cheaper rendering path. `game/graphicsSampling.ts` excludes those frames and the first second after returning outside from Auto calibration and quality recovery. Doorway transitions reset the sampling windows; pending Auto resolution increases wait until the outdoor camera settles. Manual quality selection and FPS reporting remain available indoors.

Tyre deformation retains its existing bounded CPU atlas, page recycling and wheel stamps. `ui/world/trackTextureUpload.ts` uploads only changed RGBA rectangles using the Three 0.180 renderer's texture binding state. This avoids the synchronous GPU-state queries in `copyTextureToTexture`, which otherwise stalled exploration while the autonomous rover stamped tracks. Uploads reset row/skip unpack state before returning; texture ownership and disposal remain with the track system. Regression tests compare the uploaded atlas with the CPU data across wrapped stamps and recycled pages.

`prepareWorldMaterials` compiles and draws loaded meshes into a one-pixel viewport during loading, including terrain and furnishings behind the initial camera. Auto also prepares the shadowed High/Ultra output path (linear HDR when supported, otherwise the canvas) before restoring its initial Performance state; an explicitly selected Performance startup skips these heavier variants. A shadow draw and composer draw prime depth and effect programs. The helpers restore render targets, frustum culling, viewport and shadow update state even on failure and do not dispose borrowed targets. This moves first-use GPU work under the existing loading cover without changing detail, draw distance or steady-state rendering. Hidden objects stay hidden.

A November 15, 2026 headless Chrome check at 1280 × 720, Performance quality, measured approach/entry/exit 95th-percentile frame times of 150/183/100 ms before these changes and 67/100/50 ms afterward. The final approach–entry–turn–exit trace had no initial geometry-buffer uploads or JavaScript long tasks; the former first-turn maximum of 317 ms fell to 117 ms. These are development-machine observations, not a device FPS guarantee. Detailed interiors remain GPU-heavy. A real WebGL comparison verified byte-identical partial/wrapped track updates and page clearing against the former copy path, including interleaved ordinary Three texture uploads.

An Ultra check previously recorded 19 cold shader links and 3.1–4.7-second pauses after switching from Auto. Preparing the device's shadowed output path removed those links and initial geometry uploads from the same route; approach/turn maxima were 133/150 ms, with a separate 306 ms entry spike. The final Ultra run had no page/shader/request errors and restored outdoor effects and camera after exit. Scene preparation measured 32.1 seconds on this development machine (22.9 seconds before the extra variants). These are noisy individual startup observations, not controlled load-time or real-phone benchmarks. GPU preparation is deliberately moved under the loading cover; explicit Performance skips the higher-tier preparation.

Detail distances are 320 metres in Performance, 420 in the Auto preset, 520 in High and 650 in Ultra. Auto's measured selection uses the chosen preset's distance. Performance has no post-processing and hides one outer detail ring while retaining nearby collision contacts. The base ground is divided into independently culled 70-metre patches; the far region retains its 256 × 192 grid in 48 cullable patches. Shared edges within each surface remain continuous. The one-metre base joins the two-metre detail tiles through narrow, triangulated edge stitches; contact sampling follows those same triangles.

The mountain horizon has a separate 2,588-metre range, sufficient to see across the bounded map. `marsRegionalLandscape.ts` tests its existing patches against the final camera frustum and skips patches completely covered by ready detailed terrain. `detailDistanceCulling.ts` retains the original tier far plane for equipment and detailed geometry without changing authored visibility or scene lights. It reuses current world bounds once per frame, including in rover/first-person views; Three.js still handles side/back culling and nearby shadow casters. Intentionally uncullable dynamic effects retain their existing behavior. Shared height-dependent terrain haze lets elevated ridges emerge above surface dust without changing heights, collision boundaries or map bearings. Sun, Earth and stars depth-test behind the relief. All shader variants are prepared during loading. With `?debug=1`, the world element exposes `horizonDistance`, `visibleHorizonPatches`, `horizonPatches` and `culledDetailObjects`; `viewDistance` continues to mean the detail budget, and ready tile-pool counts are not draw counts.

Sun shadows use 1024-pixel maps in the Auto preset and 1536 in High/Ultra. Ultra retains the highest resolution cap, longest view distance and full effects. The helmet lamp fades in smoothly at dusk, reaching its existing brightness at midnight. Its shadow map stops updating when unlit, while light counts and compiled material variants stay stable.

`game/explorationBounds.ts` restricts tile generation to the real MOLA crop and places four impassable collision walls at the foot of an authored mountain perimeter. Walls have no climbable top, so jumping or driving cannot cross them. The playable interior is about 1.74 × 1.32 km; all seven landmarks remain reachable. The bounded tile pool supplies local detail within this region, not infinite map expansion. Two-metre detail sampling, cached height halos, incremental normals/bounds and nearest-first generation reduce tile completion spikes. `createStreamedMars.coverage()` only hides the far terrain where a complete ring of visible detail tiles has finished; both fine ground and rocks use that same cached rectangle. Prepared collision contacts remain active while the rest of a ring loads. Track pages and effect pools remain bounded.

Curiosity keeps global destinations but filters steering/contact probes to a local obstacle set. Its broad phase measures box extents so distant perimeter walls cannot deflect its patrol. The world reuses obstacle buffers for walking, driving and LiDAR. `MarsWorld` is memoized with stable callbacks; HUD updates run at 8 Hz. Curiosity's periodic browser-storage writes run during idle time, while page hide and teardown still save immediately.

The FPS meter is an optional user feature, not a stray debug statement. `?debug=1` exposes detailed diagnostics; `sitePreview=perseverance`, `sitePreview=outpost`, `sitePreview=outpost-lights`, `sitePreview=boundary` and `landingPreview=16.95` are controlled visual checkpoints that require `debug=1`. The boundary checkpoint faces the eastern mountain wall; the lighting checkpoint faces the exterior crew corridor. Ordinary links omit these parameters. Diagnostic DOM attributes include load time, calibration trials, active quality, render scale, terrain counts and view distance.

## Asset preparation and hosting

`lib/assetUrl.ts` prefixes shipped asset paths for both the Site origin and the `/Tarukh/` Pages subpath. Preserve it when changing loaders. Keep the Draco WASM and wrapper: the rover and astronaut loaders, asset-preparation checks and tests use them.

- `node scripts/prepare-mars-region.mjs` reproduces the local MOLA crop and WebP overview from the NASA PDS product. Downloads go to ignored `outputs/mola/`; none occur during gameplay.
- `node scripts/prepare-mars-surfaces.mjs <source-directory>` rebuilds credited Poly Haven derivatives from verified source maps and their manifest.
- `scripts/prepare-rover.mjs` documents its pinned offline glTF tools. It retains all source triangles while partitioning the wheels and removing unused texture copies.
- `scripts/prepare-kepler-rover.mjs <source.glb> <tools-directory>` fits the supplied furnished cabin to the same vehicle dimensions and reuses the exact prepared six-wheel rig. The shipped derivative needs no source file or preparation tools during normal builds. Its five distinct screen materials and mesh-free driver anchor must survive compression; `keplerAsset.test.mjs` checks these and compares all tire/upright triangles and pivots with the original rig.
- `ui/world/keplerCockpit.ts` binds those five screens to `game/roverInstruments.ts` and existing rover/LiDAR state. Displays refresh at 4 Hz while driving or viewing the cockpit and 1 Hz when parked. The history uses 64 preallocated samples. Cabin illumination is a fixed material fill with no added scene lights; owned materials, canvas textures and pending image readiness are cleaned up on disposal. The original procedural cockpit is the asset-failure fallback. Vehicle physics, damage, wheel animation and garage logic remain unchanged.
- In Mobile controls, the collapsed SAT-LINK panel sits above the rover's pedals with a 44-pixel tap area. Its expanded view and desktop placement are unchanged.
- Every successful rover boarding selects first-person through the shared keyboard/button/touch handler and clears pending building-camera restoration. The existing five-second boarding popup includes the V camera-switch hint, with camera-button wording for touch controls. Manual view switching and driving physics remain unchanged.
- The existing astronaut occupies the Kepler port seat while driving. `game/roverSeating.ts` defines its measured pelvis/eye anchors and seated limb pose. `createRiggedAstronaut.ts` aligns the thigh sockets to that anchor once per boarding and restores its normal gait on exit; the same actor follows chassis pitch, roll and yaw. Only the player's own helmet is suppressed in cockpit view, leaving hands/body visible; third person restores it. The procedural fallback also has bent seated legs. No second avatar asset or vehicle physics changes are introduced.
- `node scripts/measure-rover-windows.mjs <tools-directory>` measures the seven glazing contours after checking the source asset hash. Re-measure if the source model changes.

Game GLBs and WebPs, including the prepared outpost, greenhouse and garage, are checked in; ordinary install/build commands do not regenerate them or need the local authoring workspace. Review and authoring binaries remain excluded. License notices and source manifests accompany the prepared assets. Unused presentation images, old screenshots and unused template components were removed. See `ASSET_CREDITS.md` for attribution.

`vite.pages.config.ts` builds `dist-pages/`; the GitHub workflow publishes it and records `source_commit` in `version.json`. `vite.config.ts`, `worker/`, `db/`, `lib/connectors*` and `scripts/connector-preview/` support the separate Sites build. Their dependencies may be unused by the static game while still required by that integration. Do not remove them based solely on the active UI import graph.

## Persistence and shared systems

### Rover garage contract

The garage stands at `(26, -26)`, facing south, on the same ground datum as the campus. Its flat game-terrain pad includes the six solar panels, ramp and return bay, with a nine-metre blend into surrounding terrain. No review landscape or duplicate rover is imported. The existing crew rover starts inside at `(26, -27.3)`. Curiosity and Perseverance retain their existing roles.

`game/roverGarage.ts` updates that same `RoverVehicle` through opening, rollout, closing and return states. Driving/entry is disabled only while parked or docking. Return requires the rover to be stopped, aligned in the painted bay and unoccupied; either heading is accepted. Its actual suspension and wheel rotation follow the deck/ramp. Battery, damage and upgrades are preserved. E and the contextual touch button invoke the same action. Pausing freezes the mechanism; blocked vehicle paths pause movement, and door occupancy prevents closure. The source canopy motion is played from `Garage_Door_Open`, rather than replaced with a different door animation.

The garage incorporates an integrated base battery inverter system, automated maintenance bay repair, and rover charging dock powered by the base solar panel array (`garage-solar-racks`, 1200W peak) and a 6000Wh base battery storage bank. The inverter operates with 96% efficiency and exposes `inverterWatts`, `inverterStatus` (`'CHARGING'`, `'INVERTING'`, or `'STANDBY'`), `chargingSource` (`'base-solar'` or `'base-inverter'`), `repairActive`, and `chargerActive`. When the rover is parked inside (`state.phase === 'parked'`), damaged rover components (all wheels, cabin roof, solar array) automatically repair in the maintenance bay up to 100%, and the charging dock engages at 850W (0.35%/s battery replenishment, dynamic range update). In daylight, base solar panels recharge base storage and supply the rover directly (`chargingSource: 'base-solar'`); at night, the inverter draws from the battery bank (`chargingSource: 'base-inverter'`). Diagnostics publish `garageInverterWatts`, `garageInverterStatus`, `garageBatteryWh`, `garageRepairActive`, `garageChargingSource`, and `garageDockPopup` to the DOM dataset. A dismissable docking popup banner notifies the user of active base solar charging and automated repair (5-second display window upon docking), and the cockpit HUD reflects base solar and repair status.

`scripts/prepare-garage.mjs` derives `public/models/garage/garage.glb` from the reviewed standalone shelter without modifying it. Offline tools are glTF Transform 4.2.1, meshoptimizer 0.22, Sharp 0.35.5 and draco3dgltf 1.5.7, installed in the ignored asset-tool workspace. Static parts are grouped by material, textures become WebP capped at 1024 pixels, and geometry uses Draco compression. The browser uses the existing bundled Draco decoder. The high-detail interior is hidden behind a closed opaque door and culled at 17 metres in Performance or 24 otherwise. Two nearby non-shadowing lights share the simulated garage supply; Performance retains emissive fixtures without those extra lights. Light variants are selected before shader preparation. Geometry, materials, textures, mixer and decoder workers are disposed on teardown.

`?debug=1&sitePreview=garage` opens a reproducible approach checkpoint. Diagnostics expose the garage phase, door fraction, blockage and existing crew-rover position; they create no extra vehicle. The prepared garage binary is included with the integration.

### Environmental Radiation Shielding & Notification Contract

Environmental protection states dynamically mitigate Solar Energetic Particle (SEP) solar flare radiation based on player location:
- **Base Shielding (100% Mitigation)**: On foot, outpost rooms (`outpost.indoors`), greenhouses (`greenhouses.indoors`), the garage (`garageIndoors`) and the landing-habitat zone (`(0, 12)`, radius 10.5m) provide simulated protection. `isBaseShelter` combines existing facility detection with that landing zone. The former regolith dome, beacon, collider and shelter zone are removed; its ground is ordinary exposed terrain. Sheltered damage is zero and the existing exposure meter recovers at −1.5 units/s. These are simplified gameplay rules, not physical shielding calculations or removal of a real accumulated dose.
- **Rover Shielding (50% Mitigation)**: Triggered when the player boards the 6-wheel rover (`inRover = true` via `rover.driving`). While inside the pressurized rover chassis, a 50% mitigation multiplier (0.5x damage) applies to the radiation drain logic and exposure accumulation rate.
- **Base Shielding Notification**: Transitioning into the base interior triggers a top/center high-tech CSS UI popup displaying `SHIELD ACTIVE: 100% (HABITAT)`. It remains visible for exactly 5 seconds using `setTimeout`, then completely fades out.
- **Rover Shielding Notification & HUD Handoff**: Boarding the rover triggers a top/center UI popup displaying `SHIELD ACTIVE: 50% (ROVER)`. After 5 seconds via `setTimeout`, it animates fading out and minimizing downward into a permanent shield indicator (`.sv-rover-shield-status`) on the GTA V-style Rover HUD displaying `50% ROVER` and `0.5x DRAIN`. Exiting the rover immediately removes this indicator and resets handoff state.
- **Simulation Pure Rules**: `isBaseShelter`, `getEnvironmentalShielding`, `calculateExposureDelta`, and `calculateRadiationDrain` reside in `game/spaceWeatherService.ts` with test coverage in `tests/spaceWeather.test.mjs`. Shielding states are immutable shared values, avoiding per-frame allocation; boarding/exiting notifications are triggered once by the simulation transition.

### Outpost asset and collision contract

The cultivation district surrounds the campus with two greenhouses west, two east and two north. `GREENHOUSE_SCALE` is 1.6: the shared model, doors, furniture footprints, floors, ramps, camera limits and visibility bounds use the same scale. The buildings are approximately 17 metres across, with about a metre of clearance at the circular growing-bed entrance. Plant beds opt into their convex footprints for walking rather than oversized bounding circles; other game obstacles retain their existing collision behavior. The reachable central grow computer has a solid measured footprint.

`marsOutpostHeight` includes the enlarged pads at the shared campus ground level; overlapping blend shoulders preserve each flat foundation. Game rocks are cleared from the greenhouse footprints and ramps. The source contains a ceramic deck but no separate planetary landscape. Deck surfaces sit approximately 0.52 metres above the pad datum; 3.84-metre metal ramps bridge to game ground. Site centres and the driving lanes between buildings are unchanged.

The prepared `public/models/greenhouse/cultivation.glb` is loaded once and instanced across all six sites. Interiors are culled at 12 metres in Performance and 18 otherwise; shared shell batches remain visible farther away. Per-building frustum culling excludes off-screen instances. Materials and textures are shared, with at most 1024-pixel textures. The supplied lights are represented by their emissive fixtures to avoid adding 42 scene lights. Sliding leaves use the source's opening offsets and the existing gate interaction/safety rules. `sitePreview=greenhouse` with `debug=1` provides a reproducible approach checkpoint.

`node scripts/prepare-greenhouse.mjs <source-file> <gltf-tools-directory> <sharp-package-directory>` reproduces the greenhouse derivative and its collision layout. Offline tools are glTF Transform 4.2.1, meshoptimizer 0.22 and Sharp 0.35.5. Its defaults use the existing ignored local preparation workspace; this is not a build-time or browser dependency. Original source files are read only.

The outpost uses metre units at `(0, -65)` near the landing zone. `marsOutpostHeight` blends a level foundation into the existing terrain across a 12-metre transition. It is used by the visible ground, streaming and collision sampling. The GLB's presentation ground and scattered basalt are removed; local game rocks are cleared from the footprint.

The checked-in `public/models/outpost/campus.glb` is prepared from the reviewed v9 model. `scripts/prepare-outpost.py` samples the reviewed Blender model into `layout.json`; `scripts/prepare-outpost.mjs` creates the compressed game derivative using its documented offline tools. Neither ordinary build commands nor the browser download source models or run Blender.

`layout.json` contains a 0.25-metre floor-height grid, static collision rectangles sampled from the model, and matching gate clip names. Door collider slots remain stable for streamed terrain and become `disabled` only when fully open; LiDAR ignores disabled colliders. Closing reverses if the astronaut occupies the doorway. E and the visible gate button share the same action. Walkable decks and the ramp override ground height; nearby walls constrain the third-person camera. Conservative room-height proxies limit indoor jumps.

Outpost gates automatically close after two fully open, unobstructed seconds. The simulation clock stops while paused; no browser timer drives a door. West, east, north, botany and observatory doors are paired: an opening request closes the opposite leaf first, then opens the requested gate only once its partner is fully down. The cycling prompt can cancel that request. Occupancy uses the moving leaf plus player clearance, leaving standing space within the short botany chamber. The main entrance closes automatically without a paired gate. Greenhouse doors retain their existing manual behavior. This is a door interlock, not a pressure/fluid simulation.

The debug-only checkpoint `?debug=1&sitePreview=airlock&airlock=observatory` starts inside the selected chamber; `west`, `east`, `north` and `botany` are also supported. `observatory-room` faces the same gate from inside the dome for visual comparisons. `data-outpost-door-states` exposes amount, target, waiting, obstruction and clear-time values for walkthrough validation. Normal play does not set this diagnostic attribute.

`scripts/outpost-repairs.mjs` runs before asset batching to cut the old observatory glazing trim out of the open doorway and fill the gaps around the ten internal gate frames. Fitted pressure collars preserve a 1.79 × 2.22 metre clear opening and the original raised-leaf pocket, use existing materials, and keep dark interior faces separate from the white exterior shell. The original review model, animation clips and collision layout are retained; existing side colliders already cover the added infill.

Shell sectors remain visible farther than furnishing sectors (8 metres in Performance, 14 otherwise). Furnishings do not cast outdoor sun shadows. Shared meshes are instanced, small static fittings are joined by material, and textures are capped at 1024 pixels. Reflective transparent glazing avoids the review model's scene-wide refraction pass. The loader uses bundled Meshopt decoding, prepares animations during scene loading and disposes meshes, instances, textures, mixer and lights on unmount. Auto starts at its probe's actual Performance candidate so calibration remains eligible with the added scene.

`ui/world/instanceVisibility.ts` also culls individual static furnishing instances against the final camera frustum. `outpost.updateVisibility(camera)` runs after camera positioning. It preserves original transforms/colors and the aggregate mesh bound, reuses scratch arrays and only uploads matrices when the visible instance list changes. Instance spheres include building transforms and conservative edge padding. Animated gates and collision geometry are unaffected. Controllers are released on teardown. This avoids submitting a whole room's furniture when only a few instances are in view.

`ui/world/interiorLighting.ts` links a steady diffuse room fill to cloned interior and animated gate materials. Ultra additionally restores cool-white/violet metallic highlights using two local GGX specular terms on interior-sector materials only, reusing the historical room centers. Shared uniforms and cached view-space vectors avoid scene lights, extra passes and doorway recompilation. Gate-only clones, exterior walls, ground and other buildings receive no added specular term; authored emissive fixtures remain unchanged. Gate receivers are resolved from the configured animation tracks, independently of static sector classification. Textures remain shared, and only cloned materials are owned by the lighting helper; the outpost loader still disposes source resources.

`ui/world/outpostRendering.ts` retains a 250 ms exit grace to avoid boundary flicker. Ultra restores indoor GTAO contact shading and bloom at their previous strength; Auto/High keep the simpler output/FXAA room path, and Performance keeps direct rendering. Auto uses this policy when its measured effective tier is Ultra. Reduced motion disables bloom. Existing sun shadow maps stay cached indoors and the helmet spotlight stays off while room lighting is available. Outdoor shadow updates resume on departure without changing shader variants; geometry, texture detail and selected resolution are retained. The `indoorRendering` diagnostic reports `ultra`, `simple` or `outdoor`.

Ultra's `BoundedGTAOPass` reuses the preceding scene pass's depth texture and reconstructs normals, avoiding a second geometry draw and visibility traversal. Its half-resolution output remains capped at 768 pixels on the longest side. Horizon pixels beyond the selected tier's original detail distance skip occlusion sampling; extending mountain visibility does not extend that shading budget. Auto/High retain the normal-buffer path; absent scene depth also falls back to that path. Both shader variants are warmed during loading. Composer ping-pong changes only update uniform references, and borrowed depth remains composer-owned. `BoundedBloomPass` caps the first bloom mip at 512 pixels, preserves the authored strength/radius/threshold, and reuses blur uniforms and equally sized buffers through resolution changes. These are rasterized PBR and screen-space effects, not hardware ray tracing.

A 1280 × 720 headless Chrome Ultra walkthrough measured 872 → 547 draw calls at the outpost approach after depth reuse. Restoring indoor effects changed the stationary room sample from 227 to 244 calls, with the same geometry plus fullscreen draws; mean frame time increased from 74.7 to 88.0 ms indoors, while approach mean decreased from 61.8 to 56.1 ms. Neither route linked shaders or uploaded initial geometry buffers during play. These are individual development-machine samples; Ultra intentionally adds indoor shading cost, and physical-device performance still needs verification.

`game/curiositySave.ts` keeps the existing Curiosity storage key and validation. Previously stored rover state remains compatible. Removed mission saves are not read or erased. Returning to controls setup starts a new player/crew-rover session.

The renderer retains Mars locomotion, terrain contacts, rover simulation, all three rover models, landing, both cameras, touch controls, map/LiDAR, reduced-motion handling, quality selection and WebGL recovery. The original astronaut fallback and its materials live in `createFallbackAstronaut.ts` and `suitMaterials.ts`.
