# Scientific and simulation assumptions

The game uses public scientific data for regional context and authored rules for play. The sources in [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) do not validate its speeds, animations, operating procedures.

## Regional terrain

The supplied Mars Trek extent uses an equirectangular projection centred at 134.973236° E, 14.089500° N. North/south uses 12 game metres per degree; east/west includes the cosine of the central latitude. Horizontal scale is approximately 1:4,940 near the centre, with increasing distortion farther away. Elevations are divided by 480 relative to the local landing datum, giving about 10× relief exaggeration relative to horizontal distance.

The 16-pixel/degree MOLA product includes measured and interpolated altimetry. The downsampled crop has roughly 0.316° × 0.318° cells, about 19 km north/south before gameplay compression. It cannot resolve individual rocks, narrow channels or an actual landing pad. Surface grains, local rocks and flattened spawn areas are artistic additions.

Landmarks mark gazetteer feature centres, not necessarily summits or boundaries. Exploration is restricted to the supplied MOLA region: approximately 1.74 × 1.32 game kilometres inside an authored mountain perimeter. These perimeter ridges and their impassable collision walls are gameplay boundaries, not measured Martian features. Detail tiles are only generated where they intersect the survey crop. All seven landmark positions are retained. Perseverance's coordinates mark its historical touchdown; the model and surrounding rocks are original approximations, not a live position feed or measured reconstruction.

## Movement, vehicles and time

The solar-powered garage is a fictional shelter sized around the existing crew rover. Its six panels are assigned 200 W each, with a 6 kWh battery initially holding 4.2 kWh, 170 W of lighting/control demand and a 650 W door motor. The parked rover can recharge through the 850 W dock and 96%-efficient base inverter; its authored 0.35-percentage-point/second charge rate compresses charging time. These are game assumptions, not supplied panel specifications or NASA ratings. Automatic rollout and parking use a slow, safeguarded straight approach; this is not a validated autonomous docking or pressure-control simulation. Parking does not repair or reset the crew rover. The garage battery and docking state last for the current play session only.

Six fictional food-cultivation modules surround the base. Their mature plants, propagation racks and water equipment come from the supplied enclosed greenhouse asset. Six is a layout/design choice, not a claim that the displayed crops feed a specified crew. In Free Exploration these are explorable facilities without resource or harvesting mechanics. Story adds a separate, labelled planning forecast for two greenhouses; the displayed mature plants are retained art, not measured growth. Door pressure and closed-loop life-support performance are not physically simulated.

The fictional outpost is placed at human scale on an artistically graded patch of the game's terrain. Its plan, furniture, lights and lifting gates come from the separately reviewed game model, not measured Martian infrastructure. Floors and wall collisions use sampled geometry; indoor headroom uses conservative room-height proxies. Gates operate independently with an occupancy safeguard, without pressure simulation or automatic airlock sequencing. Adding this model does not change the existing radiation-shielding rules for the shuttle and bunker.

Mars movement uses an approximate 3.71 m/s² gravity and an authored launch speed. The suit poses, gait, landing compression and camera response are animation choices, not validated EVA biomechanics. Airborne horizontal velocity persists until collision or landing; short-hop air drag is neglected.

The crew rover's 50 km/h forward and 10 km/h reverse limits, acceleration, braking, steering and mud response are gameplay settings. They are not NASA vehicle specifications. Suspension contacts follow the game's rendered terrain and traversable rocks. Curiosity's patrols and scan effects are simulated; Perseverance is stationary.

LiDAR is a 2D collision scan: 180 horizontal beams over 30 metres, at up to 8 scans per second, with 0.25-metre sampling steps. It uses the driving collision boundaries, excludes traversable stones and does not apply automatic brakes. Finite angular resolution and the 2D approximation limit detection. It is not a reproduction or certification of NASA sensing hardware.

The opening is a 32-second, SpaceX-inspired Earth recovery montage before Mars exploration. A covered cut omits orbital coast; hot-staging, Starship's landing flip, sequential engine shutdown and a tower catch are time-compressed presentation. Dimensions, lift-pin locations, contact altitude, exhaust and dust are authored approximations, not flight telemetry or fluid dynamics. The existing shuttle remains parked as the Mars shelter. A complete visual day/night cycle takes ten active minutes; pause and the opening stop that cycle. This is not a real Martian sol, a survival deadline or a resource simulation.

## Space weather and radiation exposure

Real-world radiation dose accumulates with exposure. NASA reported an average 0.67 mSv/day at Gale Crater for August 2012–June 2013; a separate NASA comparison reports about 0.213 mGy/day absorbed dose. Those units and observation periods differ; neither value is a universal quiet-Mars constant. See [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md). The Free Exploration exposure display uses authored, accelerated game rules:
- Baseline EVA exposure rate: `+0.05 mSv/s` (representing unshielded ambient cosmic radiation).
- Active SEP storm exposure rate (`sepFluxPFU >= 10`): `0.6 + Math.log10(sepFluxPFU) * 1.4 mSv/s` (scaling logarithmically with proton flux up to extreme storm levels).
- Habitat shielding: the landing-habitat zone (`(0, 12)`, 10.5-metre radius), outpost interiors, greenhouses and garage use 100% simulated protection. The crew rover uses 50% protection. These fixed percentages and the sheltered exposure-meter recovery of `-1.5 mSv/s` are gameplay rules, not calculated shielding performance or removal of a real accumulated radiation dose. The former standalone bunker has been removed, including its shelter zone.
- Radiation-themed feedback includes vision blur (`backdrop-filter: blur()`), a red peripheral vignette, camera-like static, synthetic Geiger clicks, a 30% movement speed penalty at meter value 70, and health reduction above 75. These are arbitrary game thresholds and visual effects, **not clinical thresholds or predictions of acute radiation sickness**. Their displayed mSv label does not make the model a valid medical dose-response calculation.

## Separate Starbase preview

The standalone preview follows the dimensions and vehicle configuration in the supplied concept brief, rather than asserting that it reproduces current SpaceX hardware. The 32-second catch is an authored, frame-rate-independent path: descent, hover, arm closure and settling onto the lift pins. Pin-to-rail contact and hydraulic linkage endpoints are geometric constraints. Gravity, aerodynamic forces, fuel, structural loads, collisions outside that scripted contact, and real launch procedures are not modeled. Engine control scales visual exhaust and lighting; it does not calculate acceleration. Upper-stage separation is a presentation animation. This study is separate from Mars gameplay and its scientific terrain data.

## Graphics measurements

Auto targets 40 FPS with a 43 FPS trial threshold and a 90th-percentile frame-time check. Measurements happen after entry and remain in memory. A short probe cannot certify performance under battery saver, thermal throttling, different browser refresh limits or later scene complexity. GPU names do not set the maximum graphics tier; the bounded terrain pool is a separate budget.

Performance's 800-game-metre far plane and reduced detailed-tile ring are rendering choices. They do not bound exploration or describe real Martian visibility. Optional shader preparation can still produce a driver-dependent hitch. Desktop viewport emulation does not measure real-phone performance.

## Elysium Outpost story mode

Story mode adds a fictional commissioning exercise alongside Free Exploration. Kaelen Reeves, the outpost's 14-month construction history, its initial 73% solar capability and 12% rover charge, and the seven-month transfer are authored narrative details. The Earth launch/recovery montage is labelled as Earth activity; its tower catch is not a Mars landing. Mission Control is a human Flight Director presented through text. Terminal text and science notes are neutral interface information.

The circuit and brushing mini-games are simplified maintenance exercises, not real electrical diagrams or approved cleaning procedures. Matching four labelled wires demonstrates restoring a connection after isolation. The cleaning threshold (85% overall and 70% per quadrant) is a game completion rule, not a measured solar-efficiency model. Cleaning restores available sunlight collection; it does not create an immediate battery charge. Errors and cancellation do not consume resources, and practice replays do not grant rewards.

The additional tablet workbenches represent maintenance with deliberately small puzzles. Token counts, gauge references, pipe shapes, trial-plant targets and shielding layers are authored learning controls, not equipment specifications. Water treatment is a simplified multi-stage process; oxygen generation separates water into oxygen and hydrogen, while air filters remove carbon dioxide separately. Cabin ventilation recirculates retained cabin air rather than admitting the Martian atmosphere. Plant pictures respond immediately to trial controls, but real crops need time and the picture awards no instant food. Shielding reduces the illustrated particle penetration without promising complete protection. Rock sorting records visible texture and does not identify minerals or prove life. Energy allocations select the existing discrete operating plans rather than calculating electrical circuits.

The resource model is for planning, not an engineering or medical simulation. Water recovery, water electrolysis and carbon dioxide removal have separate settings and effects. Sensor faults, reference readings, maintenance duration, capacity reductions and safe-mode thresholds are invented exercises. Real regenerative scrubbers can alternate beds to continue cleaning cabin air during regeneration. The oxygen diagram uses `2 H₂O → 2 H₂ + O₂`; it does not imply that removing CO₂ produces oxygen.

Crop light/water forecasts compress growth into seven simulated sols. Lettuce, radishes and dwarf wheat have NASA research precedents, but the models do not predict real yields or a complete diet. Stored rations remain necessary. Multiple crop allocations, including minimal cultivation, are valid commissioning plans.

The fictional route choices use planning distances of 2.1, 3.4 and 4.8 km and shortened playable routes of approximately 280, 600 and 730 game metres. These route-specific values are separate from the regional map's approximately 1:4,940 compression. They are not NASA-measured trails. Damage risk, energy estimates and dust penalties are planning assumptions; actual driving still uses the existing crew rover. Curiosity's educational Sol 3057 marker is placed nearby for play, not at a measured traverse coordinate. Perseverance remains a historical Jezero landing reference.

The radiation preparedness drill uses a dated September 2017 archive summary. Its near-Earth GOES particle observation and Mars surface observations are identified separately. Outside, habitat and prepared-shelter dose comparisons use invented estimates over the same simulated interval; no conversion from Earth PFU to a real Mars dose is asserted. A shelter reduces additional exposure without removing accumulated dose. The legacy Free Exploration exposure-meter rules described above are not used as scientific estimates for this story exercise.

Resource and overnight-test steps advance by player action. There is no failure countdown, forced real-time communication wait or single-attempt ending. Safe mode explains the deficit and permits a retry without erasing the expedition's logs. Rest and charging cycles represent compressed planning steps, not a claim about real spacecraft charging times. Conservative, balanced and productive outcomes can all demonstrate readiness; the explanation should reflect the player's saved decisions and logs.

The readiness night uses configured base demand plus the active greenhouse, oxygen, water and charging allocations. Contingency savings and fault costs are deliberately simplified percentage-point resource changes, not a habitat engineering model. The two stages advance only by player actions. These parameters are authored in `content/elysiumMissions.json`.

The story reuses the existing launch montage, buildings, plants, wind-blown dust and terrain. The Earth briefing, system standby states, species labels, crop predictions and cargo note are interface presentations, not newly animated scenes or changed interior models. The quiet greenhouse pause and final camera overview are rendered in the existing world. The supplied greenhouse has opaque walls, so the final shot rises from beside its entrance rather than travelling through a wall. Dedicated wind audio, new construction-scar/crate props and camera-photo survey capture are not included; optional surveys record a station observation and improve the planning forecast. Rest is deliberate, allowing the three parallel systems missions to interleave without forcing a new sol after every log.
